## SLIDE 31: Failure Reason #1

## SLIDE 32: Failure Reason #2
**Visual:** "2. Leader uses it, team doesn't"

**[SCRIPT]**

"Failure reason number two: The leader uses it, the team doesn't.

You figure out AI. You get good at it. You're saving hours.

But your team? They're still doing everything the old way. Now YOU'RE the bottleneck. Because everything that 'needs AI' flows through you.

That's not scale. That's a different kind of trap."

---

## SLIDE 33: Failure Reason #3
**Visual:** "3. Fear of errors"

**[SCRIPT]**

"Failure reason number three: Fear of errors.

AI isn't perfect. It makes mistakes. And in accounting, mistakes matter.

So you don't trust it. You double-check everything. And eventually you think, 'This is more work, not less.'

Here's the thing: AI doesn't have to be perfect. It has to be better than the alternative. If AI gets you 85% of the way there in 10% of the time, and you review the rest... that's still a massive win."

---

## SLIDE 34: Failure Reason #4
**Visual:** "4. No feedback loop"

**[SCRIPT]**

"Failure reason number four: No feedback loop.

You try a prompt. It kind of works. You move on.

You never go back and ask: 'How could this be better? What did it miss? What would I change?'

So you never improve. Your prompts stay mediocre. Your results stay inconsistent.

The firms winning at AI? They're iterating. They're treating prompts like code—versioning them, improving them, sharing them."

---

## SLIDE 35: Failure Reason #5
**Visual:** "5. Shiny object syndrome"

**[SCRIPT]**

"Failure reason number five: Shiny object syndrome.

New AI tool every week. ChatGPT, then Claude, then Gemini, then Copilot, then the next thing.

You're always learning, never mastering. You've got 12 subscriptions and zero workflows.

The best firms pick ONE tool and go deep. Mastery beats novelty."
